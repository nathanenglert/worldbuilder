# Houses & holdings — raw notes

Unstructured on purpose. This is what a writer's notes folder actually looks like, and
what `world-from-notes` is pointed at: prose with real dates buried in it, some of it
already in canon, some of it not, some of it contradicting what is.

## Corrath

Founded 500 AR, and I've been treating that as firm. The Vale is the productive part —
the city itself is a market and a garrison and not much else.

House Vane holds it. Aldric is the fourth of that name and the one the story cares
about. His mother Maren outlived him, which I want to keep: she's the one who has to
decide what happens after the siege.

## Marrow

Wall town, eastern approach. Roughly 600 AR for the founding — I have never nailed this
down and I don't think I want to. It's the last thing standing between Vashen and the
Vale, which is the only structural fact about it that matters.

Population somewhere around nine thousand before the siege, a third of that after.

## House Ferrow — NOT YET WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE

The third house, and the one I keep forgetting to actually record.

Seat at **Greyford**, upriver from Marrow on the Silt. Older than Vane by a long way —
call it 480 AR, before Corrath was even founded, which is the point: they were here
first and they have never stopped mentioning it.

Corvin Ferrow is the current head. Born 0768. Married *into* the Vane line — his wife
was Aldric's older sister, which makes their son a claimant if Aldric's line fails. I
think this is the succession crisis I actually want.

Ferrow sat out the siege. Deliberately. That's the interesting thing about them.

## The Silt

River. Runs west out of the Rimefall hills, past Greyford, past Marrow, into the Vale.
Everything upriver of Marrow is Ferrow country in practice regardless of who claims it
on a map.

## Language

High Tongue is the administrative language, not a spoken one — nobody's mother taught it
to them. Vashen kept it after the conquest because the tax rolls were already written in
it, which I like: the empire adopting the language of the people it beat, for reasons of
pure bureaucratic laziness.

Ferrow are said to still use the older forms. Unverified. Might be a thing they claim
about themselves rather than a thing that's true.

# Twelve — The Siege

## The breach

They came when the passes were dry, exactly as Aldric had always said they would, and the
saying of it did nobody any good at all.

The gate captain watched from the third tier as the wall of Marrow opened — not fell,
opened, the way a seam opens, unhurried and without drama, a thing that had been coming
apart for two days finally agreeing to finish. The Vashen engineers had been patient. That
was the part nobody wrote down afterwards: how patient they had been.

He thought of the duke on this parapet, years ago, telling him that spring had nothing to
do with it. He had not believed it at the time. He had gone on not believing it for six
springs, and then Aldric had been right the once, and that was the once that counted.

Somebody was shouting his name from the stair. He was bleeding from somewhere above the
hairline and had not noticed.

"The tier will not hold, sir."

"No," he said. "It will not."

Behind him, down the long grey throat of the valley, the Vale of Corrath lay under its own
weather, and would go on lying there under somebody else's flag by Highsun. Three hundred
years, and about ninety seconds to decide what they had been worth.

He went down to meet them.
